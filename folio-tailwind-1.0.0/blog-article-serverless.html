Practical serverless: when to use functions, and when not to

Lessons from shipping serverless backends: latency, cold starts, and cost patterns.

Alexander McQueen

Computer Science student & full-stack developer

When serverless is a good fit

Event-driven workloads, spiky traffic, and small, isolated APIs benefit most. You trade some control for operational simplicity.

Watch the cost model

High-throughput, long-running processes can become expensive on pay-per-invocation models. Always estimate with realistic traffic.

"Serverless removes ops friction — but it doesn't remove the need to understand latency and cost."

Mitigations

  • Use provisioned concurrency for critical endpoints to avoid cold starts.
  • Cache responses and offload heavy work to background jobs.
